Project 03 · Web · Discovery flow

Music discovery for the slow listener.

We swapped a streaming service's endless infinite-scroll feed for a small set of human-curated weekly mixtapes — and watched the most engaged segment of users grow.

Role
UX strategy + Visual design
Team
3 designers, 1 motion, 4 engineers
Year
2023
Scope
Strategy · UI · Motion
The problem

The discovery feed had become a candy aisle.

Engagement was up, but a deeper look at the data was unkind: average track listen time was down 19%, save rates were flat, and qualitative interviews kept surfacing the same sentence — "I don't know what to listen to."

The discovery surface had become a wall of 80+ rails — moods, charts, made-for-you, podcasts. We were optimising for "another tap" instead of "a moment of taste."

Approach

Fewer choices. More taste.

  • Move 01

    From rails to rooms

    Replaced 80+ algorithmic rails with three weekly "rooms": Late, Loud, Lush. Curated by a small in-house editorial team.

  • Move 02

    Slow motion

    A six-frame intro animation per room. Not a loading state — an invitation to slow down before pressing play.

  • Move 03

    Mixtape, not playlist

    Each mixtape has a hand-written editor's note. Not metadata — a paragraph of taste.

Outcomes · 90 days post-launch

We optimised for taste — and the metrics caught up.

+28% Average session length on Discover.
+12% Save-to-library rate per session.
−18% Skip rate inside curated mixtape rooms.
